Straight talk on size

The video data is compressed first (best of raw / LZW / arithmetic), then painted. But a modern .mp4 or .webm is already compressed — its bytes are almost random, so every lossless method gives up and stores it raw (the pigeonhole principle: you can't losslessly fit N random bytes into fewer than N). The image ends up ≈ the video size plus a little PNG overhead — tiny for real videos, larger only for very short clips.

Why not base64-then-compress? base64 first makes the data 33% bigger; compressing then only claws that back — measured on a real 117 KB clip it came out 2% larger than the video, never smaller. It's a detour that can't win, so we compress the raw bytes directly instead.

Genuinely smaller video needs lossy re-encoding (dropping resolution / bitrate / detail) — a separate, opt-in tool. Compressible sources (screen recordings, raw/uncompressed video) do shrink here; the panel shows the method actually used.
